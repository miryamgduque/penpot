# Reference — copy and layout conventions

The rules we converged on across the pitch and deck. Apply them to new copy so it
doesn't read as bolted on.

## Voice

- **Catchy but not fake, and not commercial.** Ground every claim in the actual
  mechanism. "Rules are enforced at the write path" beats "revolutionary
  AI-powered governance".
- **Audience is half technical, half not.** Keep the technical substance, but
  define the jargon inline the first time (`a11y`, `CI`, `MCP`, `write path`,
  `BYOK`). The popovers exist precisely so the surface can stay readable while
  the detail stays available.
- **State the limit.** Conceding what the thing *can't* do ("taste cannot be
  enforced") buys more credibility than it costs, and pre-empts the obvious
  objection.
- **Don't overuse a word** because it's handy. "Honest" got flagged for exactly
  this. Vary it, keep it where it's precise.

## Punctuation and typography

- **No em dashes.** Use a colon, semicolon, comma or parentheses. En dashes are
  fine in ranges (`Sep–Oct`, `$2–4k`).
- Real ellipsis `…`, not three dots.
- Sentence case in headings.

## Structure of a slide / section

- **Brow, title and act goal must not say the same thing three times.** If the
  columns already say "More efficient / More capable", the title shouldn't also
  be "cheaper and more capable" — make it the umbrella instead.
- **Bullets: one defining sentence, detail in the popover.** A bullet should be
  scannable on its own and carry the *why*, not just the *what*. Bold the key
  phrase inside the sentence rather than prefixing a label.
- **Chain the points** where the argument is sequential — e.g. "…only one way
  into the file" → "a change that breaks a rule is refused before it lands".
- **Lead with the strongest point**, not the caveat. A bullet that opened on
  "the open question…" was rewritten to open on "the eyes already exist".

## Layout (pitch)

- **Title narrower than body.** `h2` is capped at 48rem so headings sit as tighter
  blocks above wider text. The hero `h1` is the deliberate exception.
- **Leads** are 1.12rem, except the hero lead which stays larger.
- **Backgrounds alternate** `band` / `band--surface` down the page. Adding a
  section in the middle flips every one after it — fix the whole run, or two
  same-coloured sections will merge visually.

## Framing

- **Progressions, not menus.** "Today → the near step → a future model" reads far
  better than "Option A / Option B", which implies equal-footing alternatives.
- **Mark horizon honestly.** If something is already in motion, say so; if it's
  optional, badge it. Don't let a near-term item sit in a "future" section, or a
  speculative one look scheduled.
