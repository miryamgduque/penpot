How we compare to today's design tools

The fair comparison set: tools where a designer actually creates in a design surface, using design tokens and a real design system, and where AI is built in. (Converters, token-management platforms, and generic prompt-to-UI generators were excluded, since you can't create with your own tokens and system in them.)

The pattern is consistent: all of them let you design with tokens and a design system, and all now ship AI. What none of them do is stop you (or their AI) from going off-system. That is the gap our approach fills.

The two things that set us apart

  • Enforcement, not suggestion. The others let you and their AI design with tokens; ours makes off-token work impossible for the rules you mark strict. UXPin's Merge is the closest, but that's component reuse, not a rule engine you can point at colors, spacing, or names.
  • A rule-aware agent, built in. Their AI generates; it doesn't inherit your organization / team / file rules and refuse to break them. Ours does, and any AI model you bring inherits the same rules.

Where they're genuinely ahead

Worth stating plainly so this isn't one-sided: these are mature products. Figma's library ecosystem, Framer's publish-to-web, Motiff's AI design-system detection, and UXPin's real-component fidelity are all things we don't match today. Our claim is narrow and specific: on-system by enforcement, with an agent that can't break the rules.

Knowledge as of early 2026; these tools ship features quickly, so verify current capabilities before quoting any specific. The UXPin "partly" is a real partial exception worth acknowledging rather than glossing over.
