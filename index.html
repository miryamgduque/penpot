The shift

Design guidance used to live in the AI's head, or a doc it was free to ignore.

We moved it into the design itself: your rules live in Penpot, at the organization, team, and file level. Every agent that touches the work inherits them the same way, and the strict ones are guardrails the work has to pass, not hints in a prompt.

Three kinds run through all of it, widest to strictest:

foundation
the standing brief for a file: the direction everything inherits.
skill
know-how the agent follows, like a playbook or house style.
rule
a constraint that can be watched and enforced.

In plain terms, think of a style guide the AI can't just ignore, like a spell-checker that won't let the typo through.

Every team has design rules: the right colors, the right spacing, sensible names. Normally you just hope everyone, and every AI, remembers them. This bakes the rules into Penpot itself. The AI designer reads them automatically, and for the strict ones it simply can't produce work that breaks them.

The rules are set once and shared by the whole team, they live with your files, and you talk to the AI right inside Penpot, no second app to open, nothing to keep in sync. Set the guardrails once, and anyone (or any AI) can work without drifting off the system.

What it means for users

What changes when you sit down to work.

bidirectional

The design talks back

Not a one-way prompt. The agent reads your live file and tokens; the file flags rule violations back, to the agent and to you, as you work, and batches them into one fix.

embedded

No external app to reach the tools

The chat agent is a panel next to your canvas. You don't open Claude Code or Cursor to use the design tools; they're already here, where you're designing.

no‑sync

Nothing to keep in sync

Skills live in Penpot, in the file and in the team database, seeded from the official penpot-ai-kit. No rule files to copy into each person's setup, no drift between them.

layered

Rules at every level

Manage skills & rules for the whole company, one team, or a single file. They cascade (most-specific wins) and anything marked mandatory can't be loosened below.

enforced

Guardrails, not suggestions

“Use only our color tokens” isn't advice. The write path rejects an off-token fill or stroke, no matter which AI model made it.

What it means for teams

Set the conventions once and everyone inherits them.

Tokens settled your values and components your building blocks. Neither could hold the part that lingers in people's heads: how you validate a form, what you never ship, the house style…

That layer has always lived somewhere fragile: a doc getting old, something a teammate once explained to you, or a pattern you deduced by copying what everyone else seemed to be doing.

Now one person briefs the agent once and it becomes how it behaves for everyone. New designers inherit the conventions, and nobody has to be the design police.

The closest analogy in structure

Cursor put the rules next to the code. This puts them inside the design.

If you've used Cursor, you already have the model: an AI that follows rules you've written down. Same idea, different world: Cursor governs code, this governs the design. The one difference that matters is whether those rules are advisory or enforced.

What we built

Three moving parts, one idea.

It lives in three places in Penpot: the backend, the dashboard, and the canvas, wired together so a rule you set once works the same everywhere.

The engine
Works out which of your rules apply (company, team, or file) and blocks any change that breaks a strict one the moment it's written, whatever AI made it.
The control room
A page in Penpot's dashboard to write your rules and switch them on or off, for the whole company or one team. It comes with 12 ready-made ones from Penpot's kit.
The workspace
A panel beside your canvas with two tabs: chat with the agent, or manage your rules. As you work it flags anything off-rule and hands it to the agent to fix in one pass.

The bottom line

Set once. Shared by the team. Enforced on every write.

Your rules live in the design, not in someone's head or a doc the AI can skip. Anyone can design fast, any AI you bring stays on-brand, and nobody's left cleaning up off-brand work after the fact.
